# Molecular landscape of TCGA-BRCA: an abstract

*A fictional research report written for testing. It describes the TCGA-BRCA files of release A.*

## Abstract

We analysed the 1,101 primary breast tumors in TCGA-BRCA release A (1,247 samples in total), of which 535 (48.6%) carry all five data layers: survival, mutation, copy number, RNA and protein. Luminal A was the dominant RNA subtype (n = 421; 49.9%), followed by Luminal B (n = 192; 22.7%) and Basal-like (n = 141; 16.7%). Estrogen-receptor positivity was seen in 77.2% of tumors with a known status (n=808), and progesterone-receptor positivity in 57.0% (n=699). HER2 overexpression by immunohistochemistry was uncommon (22.5%; 164 of 728 tumors with a definitive result), and triple-negative disease made up 15–17% of tumors with all three receptors known. Median age at diagnosis was 58 years (range 26–90), and 6.9% of patients (n = 76) were diagnosed before the age of 40. Most tumors were stage II at diagnosis (n = 622; 57.9% of staged tumors), whereas stage IV disease was rare (n = 20; 1.9%). PIK3CA was the most frequently mutated gene (34.5% of 791 sequenced primary tumors), just ahead of TP53 (33.4%). TP53 mutations were concentrated in Basal-like tumors (84.9%; 90 of 106 sequenced) compared with 10.3% of Luminal A tumors. MYC amplification (n = 229; 21.2% of tumors with copy-number data) was more frequent than ERBB2 amplification (n = 133; 12.3%). Across the cohort 151 deaths were recorded (13.8%; n = 1,097 with survival data), and the death rate in stage III tumors (18.0%) was roughly double that in stage I (8.7%). ER-alpha protein was higher in ER-positive than in ER-negative tumors, with a mean RPPA difference of 2.0–2.5 units (n = 698). The 2012 consensus labels matched the RNA-seq subtype call in 95% of tumors labelled by both methods (n = 508). Finally, the Ki-67 proliferation index exceeded 20% in 55–65% of Luminal B tumors, consistent with their higher proliferative drive.
