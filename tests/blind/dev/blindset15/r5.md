# Discussion

Several features of the TCGA-BRCA cohort (release A) matter when results are generalized. Our cohort skews older, with a median age at diagnosis of 58 and 344 patients aged 65 or over. Fewer than one in twenty patients were younger than 40. Basal-like tumors arose in younger patients, with a median age of 54 compared with 61 for Luminal A. The normal-like group was youngest of all, with a median age of 49. Premenopausal women outnumbered perimenopausal women by more than five to one. Male breast cancer is represented by only 14 cases, too few for separate analysis. Diagnostic slides exist for nearly all primary tumors, 1,061 of 1,101. Younger patients were more likely to carry germline BRCA1 variants. Taken together, these features argue for age-stratified analyses in future work.
