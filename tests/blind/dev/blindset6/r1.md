# Receptor status and the RNA subtype mix in TCGA-BRCA

*A fictional research summary prepared for audit practice. It describes the TCGA-BRCA files of release A.*

Hormone receptor and HER2 status remain the first lens most readers use to slice a breast cancer cohort. This note looks at how those clinical labels line up with RNA subtype in the public TCGA files.

The release contains 1,247 samples drawn from 1,101 patients, and every one of those patients contributed a primary tumor. Among primary tumors with a positive or negative ER result, 808 were ER-positive, roughly 77%. PR positivity was lower, at about 64% of tumors with a definite PR call. HER2 immunohistochemistry was positive in 164 tumors, about 23% of those scored positive or negative, while 179 were recorded as equivocal. Using tumors with definite results for all three markers, 116 of 722 were triple-negative (about 16%).

Of the 844 primary tumors with a PAM50 call, Luminal A was the most common subtype at 421, roughly half. Basal-like tumors formed the second-largest group, ahead of Luminal B. Luminal A tumors outnumbered HER2-enriched tumors by more than six to one.

ER positivity was nearly universal among Luminal A tumors (393 of 402) but was recorded in only 12 of the 136 basal-like tumors with a known ER result. Patients with ER-positive tumors also responded better to endocrine therapy than patients with ER-negative tumors.
