# Somatic mutations across breast cancer subtypes

*This is a fictional summary written for practice; it describes release A of the TCGA-BRCA files.*

Mutation calls are among the layers most often joined to clinical labels. In this brief we summarise the gene-level calls for primary tumors and compare them across RNA subtypes and histologies.

Gene-level mutation calls are available for 791 primary tumors, and none of the normal-tissue samples appear in that file. TP53 was the most frequently mutated gene, altered in 264 tumors. PIK3CA mutations were found in about 35% of the profiled tumors. Among the classic breast cancer drivers, CDH1 was mutated in 102 tumors and GATA3 in 98. MAP3K1 mutations, by contrast, turned up in roughly 8% of tumors.

Within basal-like tumors that also had mutation data, 90 of 106 carried a TP53 mutation, compared with about 10% of Luminal A tumors. PIK3CA showed the opposite pattern, with mutations in nearly half of Luminal A tumors but only 7 of the 106 basal-like tumors. Not a single basal-like or HER2-enriched tumor carried a GATA3 mutation.

CDH1 mutations were concentrated in lobular cancers, where 85 of 144 profiled tumors were mutated, roughly 52%. These TP53 mutations are what make basal-like tumors grow faster than luminal tumors.

A fuller treatment would also look at co-occurrence and mutual exclusivity between drivers.
