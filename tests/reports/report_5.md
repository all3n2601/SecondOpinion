# Test report 5: Edge cases

*A fictional summary written by the SecondOpinion team to test the auditor. Several statements are wrong on purpose.*

Of the 1,101 primary tumors, 1,080 have copy number data and 750 have protein data.
Approximately 12% of primary tumors are triple-negative.
About 77% of tumors are ER-positive and 66% are PR-positive.
ER-negative tumors are more likely to carry TP53 mutations than ER-positive tumors.
TP53 mutations are more common in Basal-like than in Luminal A tumors.
TP53 and PIK3CA are the two most frequently mutated genes.
CDH1 is mutated in about 13% of tumors.
Lobular carcinomas make up about 18% of primary tumors.
Infiltrating ductal carcinoma is the most common histological type.
Most patients were postmenopausal.
Fewer than 1% of patients are male.
ERBB2 is amplified in about 12% of tumors.
Luminal B is the second most common subtype.
Normal-like is the least common subtype.
Luminal A tumors outnumber Basal-like tumors three to one.
There are twice as many Luminal A as Luminal B tumors.
The median age at diagnosis was 61 years.
About 14% of patients died during follow-up.
Stage IV disease is present in fewer than 5% of tumors with a recorded stage.
Nearly all primary tumors have copy number data.
The majority of primary tumors have RNA-seq data.
At least 1,000 primary tumors have survival data.
HER2-positive tumors account for 164 of 728 tumors with a known HER2 status.
About 80% of triple-negative tumors are Basal-like.
Only 7 samples are metastatic.
Among triple-negative tumors, the median age was 55 years.
Patients with Basal-like tumors have worse overall survival.
Black women are more likely to have triple-negative tumors.
