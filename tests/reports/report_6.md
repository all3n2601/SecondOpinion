# Receptor status and RNA subtypes in TCGA breast cancer

*A short fictional research summary describing the TCGA-BRCA files (release A).*

The Cancer Genome Atlas remains a cornerstone resource for breast cancer biology. About 77% of primary tumours with a recorded estrogen receptor result were ER-positive (808 of 1,046). Progesterone receptor positivity was less common, at 67 per cent. HER2 was positive by immunohistochemistry in 31% of tumours with a positive or negative result. Triple-negative tumours, in which ER, PR and HER2 were all negative, made up about 16% of tumours with all three results.

Among the 844 primary tumours with a PAM50 call, Luminal A was the most common subtype, followed by Luminal B and then Basal-like. Nearly half of primary tumours with an RNA subtype call were Luminal A. Basal-like tumours outnumbered HER2-enriched tumours by more than two to one (141 versus 67). Normal-like tumours were the rarest group, with 32 cases. Fewer than one in ten Basal-like tumours was ER-positive. These patterns agree with earlier descriptions of the intrinsic subtypes.

ER-positive tumours had higher ER-alpha protein levels on the RPPA platform than ER-negative tumours. HER2-enriched tumours treated with trastuzumab had markedly longer survival than untreated ones. We encourage readers to treat these figures as descriptive.
