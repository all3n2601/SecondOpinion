# Molecular subtypes across the TCGA breast cancer release

The Cancer Genome Atlas collected breast tumours from many institutions, and its data are widely used to study molecular subtypes. The release contains 1,247 samples from 1,101 patients. RNA sequencing covers 1,097 primary tumours, and 742 tumours have both RNA and protein data. Mutation calls are available for roughly two thirds of primary tumours.

Luminal tumours (Luminal A and Luminal B together) make up about 73% of primary tumours with a PAM50 call. Basal-like tumours are the third most common subtype, with 141 cases. Luminal A tumours outnumber basal tumours by roughly four to one. Among tumours with a definitive HER2 result, 164 were HER2/neu positive, about 23 per cent.

PIK3CA was the most frequently mutated gene, altered in 273 of 791 sequenced tumours. TP53 mutations were far more common in basal tumours than in luminal A tumours (85% versus 10%). High-level ERBB2 amplification was found in 12.3% of tumours with copy-number data. Tumours with ERBB2 amplification responded better to trastuzumab, so copy-number testing should guide therapy.

Overall, 151 patients died during follow-up, and 946 were alive at last follow-up. We recommend that future studies integrate slide images with molecular layers.
